# Proposing Edits and Reporting Songs on WorshipCommons

<div class="article-intro">

WorshipCommons is improved by the people who use it. Anyone signed in can propose a change to a live song -- fix a chord, add a verse, correct a spelling, add a missing file, or transcribe a score. Nothing changes until a reviewer approves it. If a song was shared by someone who doesn't own it, or something else is wrong, you can report it.

</div>

<div class="prereqs">
<h4>Before You Begin</h4>

- Sign in to WorshipCommons. See [Your Account and Writer Page](./your-account.md).
- Open the song's page. See [Finding Songs](./finding-songs.md).

</div>

## Proposing an Edit

1. On the song page, find the **Improve it** panel and click **Propose an edit**.
2. Under **What are you proposing?**, choose one:
   - **Correction** -- Fix the lyrics, chords, or details, such as the scripture reference or key. The whole song form opens, filled in with the live song.
   - **Additional file** -- Add a file. Nothing else about the song changes. See [Adding Files](#adding-files).
   - **Master recording** -- Add the finished recording of the song under its own license. The song's license stays the same.
   - **Removal request** -- Ask a reviewer to take the song down.
3. Make your changes or add your files.
4. In the note box (**What changed, and why?**, **About these files**, **About this recording**, or **Why should this song come down?**), tell the reviewer about your change. The note needs at least 10 characters; the counter under the box turns red until it does. For a removal request, say who you are and why the song should come down.
5. Check **My change is accurate, anything I add is mine to give, and I give it under this song's license.** (Removal requests skip this box.)
6. Click the button at the bottom: **Propose this edit**, **Propose these files**, **Propose this recording**, or **Request removal**.

A reviewer compares your change with the live song and decides. Track it in **My submissions**. Once it's approved, you're credited on the song page under **Contributors & changes** on the **About & rights** tab, and the entry in **My submissions** reads **You are credited on the song page.**

:::info
Only the writer can change a song's license. When you propose a correction, the form shows **License: … Only the writer can change a song's license.** and the license can't be edited.
:::

Other places on the song page lead to the same form: **Add a master recording** in the **Improve it** panel (when the song has none), **Propose one →** under **Scripture connection** when the song has no scripture reference, and **Add it to this song →** under **Sources & outputs** on the About & rights tab when a file is missing.

### Adding Files

Choose **Additional file** to add any of these:

- **Score** -- MusicXML, MuseScore, or LilyPond (`.musicxml`, `.xml`, `.mxl`, `.mscz`, `.ly`), up to 25 MB
- **Score scan** -- A PDF or image of the printed score, up to 25 MB
- **Demo recording** -- MP3, WAV, M4A, or OGG, up to 25 MB
- **Accompaniment track** -- The music without the lead vocal, up to 90 MB
- **MIDI melody** -- A `.mid` file, up to 1 MB
- **Multitracks** -- A ZIP of stems, up to 50 MB
- **Cover art** -- JPG, PNG, or WebP, up to 20 MB
- **Lyrics or ChordPro file** -- `.cho`, `.crd`, `.txt`, or `.chordpro`, up to 1 MB

Add at least one file. If you add a recording, check **This recording is mine (or I have the owner's permission to share it).**

## Transcribing a Score

If a song has a melody file but no engraved score, you can draft one in ABC notation and send it for review. Once approved, it becomes the song's engraved score, and the **Sheet music** tab and full-score page start using it.

1. On the song page, open the **Sheet music** tab and click **No sheet music yet — help transcribe it** (or **Help turn this sheet into an editable score** when the song has a PDF). You can also click **Help transcribe it →** on the full-score page.
2. Click **Draft from MIDI**. A rough ABC draft of the melody appears in the editor, and the engraved result renders live beneath it. Any notation problems are listed in red.
3. Tidy the draft: fix rhythms and bar lines, and add the words. Open **Lyrics as w: lines (copy under each music line)** to copy the song's lyrics, already formatted as ABC `w:` lines, under the matching music.
4. Click **▶ Play original MIDI** to hear the source tune and **▶ Play my ABC** to hear your transcription. Click **■ Stop** to stop either.
5. Click **Submit for review**.

Your transcription is sent as an **Additional file** proposal with the note "ABC transcription" and appears in **My submissions** like any other change.

:::info
If the song already has an engraved score, the transcribe page says so and links to **View the sheet music →** instead.
:::

## Songs Closed to Public Edits

Some writers choose a license that keeps every change with them. On those songs, the **Propose an edit** link doesn't appear, and opening the edit page shows **The writer makes all changes to this song.** If you spot a problem, use the **Spotted a problem? Let us know.** link to report it instead.

Two groups can still propose edits to a closed song:

- **The song's publisher** -- The account that published it, including anyone the WorshipCommons team added as a co-publisher.
- **WorshipCommons reviewers** -- The team members who review submissions.

Their changes still go through review like any other edit.

## Reporting a Song

Use a report when a song was shared without the owner's permission, or when something else is wrong. You don't need to be signed in.

1. On the song page, click **Report this song** in the **Improve it** panel. (You can also click **Report a song** at the bottom of any page, or **Report** under your name in the top menu.)
2. In **Which song?**, enter the title or the song page's address. It's filled in for you when you start from a song page.
3. Under **What's the problem?**, choose a reason: **Someone shared a song they don't own**, **The words or melody are AI-generated**, **It breaks the community rules**, **The chart or files are wrong**, or **Something else**.
4. Under **What's wrong?**, explain the problem. For an ownership claim, include who owns the song and how you know; links to registrations, releases, or publisher catalogs help.
5. If you chose **Someone shared a song they don't own**, also choose **Who are you?** (the copyright owner, someone authorized by the owner, a co-writer who never agreed, or someone who recognizes the song), enter **Your name** and **Email**, check the good-faith statement, and type your full legal name as your **Signature**.
6. Click **Send the report**.

A real person will get back to you within two business days, and ownership claims are read first. When a claim holds up, WorshipCommons aims to take the song down within 48 hours. You can also email [support@churchapps.org](mailto:support@churchapps.org).

:::info
The ownership report doubles as a copyright takedown notice. If a song was taken down by mistake, the **Terms** page (linked at the bottom of every page under **Copyright / DMCA**) explains how to send a counter-notice and lists the designated copyright agent.
:::
