# Licenses and Rights on WorshipCommons

<div class="article-intro">

Every song on WorshipCommons is free for worship, but what else your church may do depends on the song. Each song page names its license and spells out, use by use, what you may do with it. This article explains the badges, the **Rights** panel, and the **Licenses** page, so you can decide in a minute whether a song works for your service, your stream, or your songbook.

</div>

:::info
WorshipCommons doesn't replace your CCLI license. Keep CCLI (and SongSelect) for the copyrighted songs you already sing.
:::

## The License Badges

The badge next to a song's title, in search results, and in collections tells you which grant the song carries:

| Badge | License | In short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Public domain** | Public domain | Free for every use, including commercial. A best-effort determination for the United States; other countries may differ. |
| **WC** | Free for worship (WorshipCommons License) | Free for worship everywhere, forever. Commercial use stays with the writer. |
| **CC BY** | Creative Commons Attribution | Free for every use, including commercial, as long as you credit the writer and link the license. |
| **CC BY-SA** | Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike | As CC BY, and anything you make from it (an arrangement, a translation, a recording) must carry the same license. |
| **CC BY-NC** | Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial | Free for worship with credit; nothing sold or monetized. |
| **CC BY-NC-SA** | Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike | As CC BY-NC, and derivatives must carry the same license. |
| **Custom** | A writer's own grant | Named on the song page, with a link to the writer's full terms. Often non-commercial, with no changes to words or melody. |

Non-commercial badges are a different color so they can't pass for free-for-worship, and the **Source** filter on the Discover page marks them with ⚠. Hover over a badge for its full name.

## Reading the Rights Panel

Open a song, click the **About & rights** tab, and scroll to **Rights**. From top to bottom:

1. **The grant** -- One sentence naming the copyright holder and the license, with a **How that works** or **Full license** link. For WorshipCommons songs it reads **Shared through WorshipCommons — free for worship everywhere, always. Commercial use stays with the writer.** Non-commercial songs add a warning that a stream with ads or a donation prompt may count as commercial.
2. **You may / You may not / You must** -- The license's plain-language deed as checklists. Public-domain songs have only a **You may** list, because nothing is forbidden.
3. **This version, use by use** -- A table with a row for **Project**, **Print**, **Stream**, **Arrange or translate**, and **Record**. Each row says **You may** or **You may not**, followed by any conditions, such as **Credit the writer and link the license**, **Non-commercial use only**, or **Share alike: release your arrangement or translation under the same license**. When a song's text, tune, translation, arrangement, recording, or artwork carry different licenses, the table combines them, so a use is allowed only when every part allows it. If a license isn't recognized, the row shows **See license** instead.
4. **The CCLI line** -- Whether a US church reports project and print use of this song to CCLI. See [CCLI Reporting](#ccli-reporting).
5. **License rows** -- One row per license in the song, labeled **License**, or **Text · Tune license** and so on when the parts differ, each linking to the license text.
6. **Attribution** -- The credit line as the writer states it. Click **Copy attribution** to paste it into a bulletin, slide, or stream description.

:::tip
The same attribution is printed automatically in the footer of every chord chart, under each chart in a service plan booklet, on the last slide of every export, and in the house-church pack, so you rarely need to copy it by hand.
:::

:::warning
The deed and the table are summaries. If they ever disagree with the license text linked from the panel, the license text controls.
:::

## CCLI Reporting

Most songs on WorshipCommons need no CCLI report: public-domain hymns, WorshipCommons songs, and Creative Commons songs are all free to project and print without reporting, and writers' custom grants usually say the same. The song page tells you in three places:

- Under the chord chart: **Free to sing, print, project and stream. No reporting required. Keep CCLI for other songs you sing.** A song that does need reporting reads **Report this song to CCLI when you use it.**
- In the **Rights** panel: **No CCLI report needed …**, or **A US church reports project and print use of this song to CCLI.**
- When the writer listed a CCLI number, a **CCLI** tag at the top of the page and the line **CCLI … — reporting is optional.** The number is there for churches that report everything they sing; nothing is owed.

The **No CCLI needed** collection (linked at the bottom of every page) lists the most-used songs that need no report and answers common questions about streaming, printing, and the rest of your catalog. See [Finding Songs](./finding-songs.md#collections).

## Streams, Recordings, and Non-Commercial Songs

Recording or streaming a worship gathering is part of worship use under the WorshipCommons License, even when the church takes an offering or the stream runs ads or a donation prompt. Selling a recording of the song, releasing it as a single or album, or using it in film, TV, ads, podcasts, or games needs the writer.

Songs under **CC BY-NC** or **CC BY-NC-SA** are stricter: the **Rights** panel warns that a monetized stream or video (YouTube ads, a paid download, a ticketed live stream) may count as commercial. If your channel is monetized, use the **Source** filter on the Discover page to stay with public-domain, WorshipCommons, and CC BY songs.

## Share-Alike Songs in Service Plans

A **CC BY-SA** song in a [service plan](./service-plans.md) is marked **CC BY-SA** in its row. Charts, stage mode, and the printed booklet always include it, but it stays out of the **House-church pack** download unless you check **Share-alike pack (include CC BY-SA songs)**, because a pack that contains a share-alike song must itself be shared under the same license.

## Arrangements and Translations

Whether you may share an arrangement or translation depends on every part of the song:

- Public-domain, WorshipCommons, and CC BY songs allow it. The song page shows **Made an arrangement or translation? Add it back.** and **Add one →** under **Translations**.
- Custom grants and no-derivatives licenses don't. The song page shows **Translations need the writer's permission.**, and the song appears as unavailable when you try to pick it as the original for a translation or arrangement.

Under the WorshipCommons License you may skip a verse, repeat a part, translate the song, or change a word so it sings, as long as you don't change what the song means. Other lyric changes need the writer. See [Sharing a Song](./sharing-a-song.md#sharing-a-translation-or-arrangement).

## The Licenses Page

Click **Licenses** at the bottom of any page (or **Questions** to jump straight to the FAQ) to open the WorshipCommons License page. It holds:

- A three-paragraph summary of the license and the full **Legal code — Version 1.0**. The legal code is the license and is always in English.
- **Who gets what** -- What your church gets and what the writer keeps.
- **Who counts as "worship"?** -- Why the grant is the writer's own permission, not a church exemption, and works the same in every country.
- **The fine print, kept small** -- Five points: the grant can never be taken back, whoever shares a song promises they own it, arrangements and translations stay in the family, credit is a kindness not a condition, and songs come as-is.
- **Releasing a song without uploading it** -- The release notice a writer can put on a chart, album, or video to release a song under the license with no upload.
- **Other licenses in this library** -- A card for each public-domain and Creative Commons license with its **You may**, **You may not**, and **You must** lists and links to the official text.
- **Common questions** -- Including whether to cancel CCLI (no), whether writers work for free, posting services on YouTube, ticketed festivals, and the difference between **Free** and **Public domain**.

Click a badge's **How that works** link on any song page to land on the matching card.
